# Specialist E — News Writing: Hard-News Report Construction Across the 18-Entry National Corpus

**Scope:** All hard-news reports (local, national, international/economic, breaking, issue/conflict) in the 18 NSPC broadcast scripts. Sports and showbiz reports are excluded from deep analysis (covered by Specialist I) but are referenced where they reuse the same report skeleton. Sources: `transcripts/Contestant-1…18/actual-script.md` (primary). Mic-check phases excluded per corpus inventory.

**Evidence labels:** `DIRECTLY OBSERVED` = quoted from a script; `CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN` = seen in ≥3 independent entries (named); `ANALYST INFERENCE` = interpretation. Transcript corrections appear as `*[intended]*` inside quotes; garbled/unclear lines are flagged.

## 2. The Universal Report Anatomy (Abstract Framework)

Derived from all 41 reports (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`, strongest in C1, C6, C7, C8, C10, C13, C14, C16, C17, C18):

```
ANCHOR CONTEXT/HOOK (optional, 1–3 lines)
   → HANDOFF (reporter name + location/beat)  ["Live sa Ormoc City, si Mica Ruor" — C2]
   → LEAD (first sentence: who/what + verb + place + time)
   → SOURCE (attribution: "Ayon sa…", named official, or embedded)
   → ESSENTIAL DETAIL (identity, numbers, statute)
   → SUPPORTING DETAIL / CONTEXT (history, comparison, cause)
   → IMPACT or CONSEQUENCE (agency response, public advisory)
   → [Q&A EXTENSION] (anchor asks; reporter answers 1–2 sentences)
   → SIGN-OFF (reporter name + station/beat formula)
   → ANCHOR THANK-YOU  ["Maraming salamat, Fier" — C6]
```

Not every report has every slot, and order flexes (Section 4). But the **five load-bearing slots — LEAD → SOURCE → ESSENTIAL DETAIL → IMPACT → SIGN-OFF — appear in 38 of 41 reports** (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`). The three deviations: C5's WPS report (no source, no impact verb), C11's local report (structure dissolves into unintelligibility), C2's national report (anchor fragments without source).

## 3. Story-Type Frameworks

### 3.1 LOCAL CRIME / ARREST REPORT (14 entries) — the most standardized story in the corpus

Canonical build, assembled from C1, C4, C6, C7, C8, C13, C14, C15, C16, C18 (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`):

```
1. ANCHOR WARNING/TEASER   "Huwag tayong maloko agad dahil arestado ang lalaki…" (C4)
                           "kahit sa gitna ng krisis, may mga mapagsamantala" (C8)
2. HANDOFF (name + live location)  "Live sa Ormoc City, si Mica Ruor" (C2)
3. ARREST LEAD (passive verb + agency + age + crime + date + place)
                           "Timbog sa ikinasang operasyon ng Ormoc City Cyber Response Team
                            ang isang scammer matapos magbenta ng ilegal na ATM cards…" (C1)
4. IDENTIFICATION (alias + age)
                           "Kinilala ang suspek na si alyas '20 anyos'…" (C1);
                           "kinilala sa pangalang Samboy" (C18)
5. LEGAL FRAME (statute)
                           "kabilang ang paglabag sa Republic Act 12010 o Anti-Financial
                            Accounts Scamming Act" (C4); "Section 5 of Republic Act 12010" (C12)
6. STATUS + PUBLIC ADVISORY
                           "Sa ngayon, nasa kustodiya na ng pulisya ang suspect" (C4);
                           "Hinihikayat ng awtoridad ang publiko na mag-report agad" (C16)
7. Q&A EXTENSION (anchor asks about police action / cases / suspect status) — C1, C2, C7, C8, C13, C14
8. SIGN-OFF → 9. THANKS
```

**Lead-verb inventory (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`):** "Timbog" (C1), "Nasikop" (C4), "Naaaresto" (C8), "Inaresto" (C12), "Matagumpay na nahuli" (C18), "Himas-rehas ngayon" (C16), "Rehas, yan ang sinapit" (C15), "Kulong" (C7 anchor). The pattern: **arrest verb first, in the passive, agency after the verb, full chronology (age → crime → date → city) compressed into sentence one.** C16 and C15 add a figurative prison idiom ("Himas-rehas", "Rehas") as a vivid lead — a deliberate, memorable device (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`, only 2 entries).

**Variation — warning-first lead:** C9, C11 and C14 invert the order: the report *opens* with the police warning ("nagbabala ang mga otoridad… maaaring kasuhan ang sinumang magbebenta ng ATM cards" — C14), and the arrest itself becomes the second sentence ("Ito ay matapos arestuhin…" — C14). This is the only recurring inversion in local leads (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`, 3 entries). It works as a public-service frame but costs the report its hook: the most newsworthy element (arrest) is demoted (`ANALYST INFERENCE`).

### 3.2 NATIONAL / SECURITY REPORT (WPS cyanide; 14 entries)

Canonical build, from C1, C6, C7, C10, C13, C17, C18 (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`):

```
1. CONTEXT (tension frame)
   "mainit-init ang tensyon ngayon sa West Philippine Sea" (C1);
   "Sa nagpapatuloy na tensyon sa pagitan ng China at Pilipinas…" (C17)
2. ACTION LEAD (seizure or order first)
   "Inatasan ng National Security Council ang Armed Forces at Coast Guard…" (C1);
   "Nakumpiska ng mga sundalong Pilipino ang sampung bote ng cyanide…" (C13);
   "Pinaigting ng NSC ang pagbabantay…" (C7)
3. SOURCE (agency or named official)
   "Ayon sa National Security Council…" (C1, C6, C9, C12, C17);
   "Dagdag pa ni Cornelio Valencia…" (C7, C8); "ayon kay Juniper Malinao ng NBI" (C8)
4. CONFIRMATION (evidence)
   "Nagpositibo naman sa cyanide ang tubig sa Spratly Islands at Ayungin Shoal" (C6);
   "kinumpirmang positibo sa cyanide ang tubig…" (C17)
5. CHARACTERIZATION (sabotahe)
   "itinuturing ito na isang sabotahe galing sa China" (C5);
   "maituturing umanong pansasabotahe ng China sa supply ng pagkain…" (C17)
6. RESPONSE (patrols; DFA protest)
   "plano nang magsumite ng ulat ang NSC sa Department of Foreign Affairs bilang
    basehan para sa diplomatikong protesta" (C17)
7. STATUS (no China reply) — C3, C13, C16
8. SIGN-OFF
```

**Deep-version variant (issue/conflict story):** C8 adds a historical-context sentence ("Dati nang nagsagawa ng armadong pag-atake ang mga Chinese Coast Guard noong June 2024…" — C8); C16 adds a legal-context sentence ("…kahit nakasaad sa international ruling na wala silang legal na batayan" — C16); C3 frames the story as accusation-versus-silence ("Itinaas na ng Pilipinas ang isyu sa China… ngunit wala pang pormal na tugon mula sa Beijing" — C3). These three are the corpus's only true **issue-based stories** — ones that add a second layer of context beyond the event (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`, 3 entries). `ANALYST INFERENCE` — the context sentence is cheap to add and raises perceived depth; C8, C16 and C3 demonstrate it without breaking the 5-minute clock.

### 3.3 INTERNATIONAL / ECONOMIC REPORT (oil + Trump; 12 entries)

Two recurring builds (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`):

**(a) Data-first (price/impact):**
```
LEAD WITH NUMBER → CAUSE → CONSEQUENCE → OFFICIAL STATEMENT → SIGN-OFF
"Sumirit na sa 43% ang dagdag sa presyo ng langis dahil sa gulong nangyayari sa Middle East.
 Pahirapan na kasing makalusot sa Hormuz ang mga barkong nag-aangkat ng petrolyo.
 Dahil dito, apektado ang ekonomiya ng buong mundo dahil sa pagtaas ng inflation rate." (C16)
```
Other data leads: "Tumaas ang presyo ng Brent crude oil sa higit isang daan at labing-isang dolyar kada bariles" (C6); "Mukhang tataas na naman kasi ang presyo ng gasolina dahil pumalo na sa $111 kada bariles ng krudo sa loob lang yan ng isang araw" (C13, conversational); "Nananatiling kritikal ang Strait of Hormuz…" (C7, status-first). The consequence triplet **"inflation, housing affordability, economic stability"** recurs verbatim in C5 and C15 — evidence of a shared source document (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`, both entries).

**(b) Statement-first (politics):**
```
ATTRIBUTION LEAD → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE
"Ayon kay US President Donald Trump, hindi niya inaasahan na labis ang magiging epekto
 ng tensyon sa ekonomiya…" (C14); "tiniyak ni US President Donald Trump na maayos umano
 ang kanilang negosasyon sa Iran" (C17); "sinabi ni US President Donald Trump na maaayos
 ang diskusyon sa pagitan ng Iran at Amerika" (C15)
```
Ten-day-ultimatum content appears in C3, C6, C10, C13, C14, C16 — always the final fact of the report (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`, 6 entries).

### 3.4 BREAKING-NEWS VARIANT

No entry runs a true breaking ticker; five entries *label* a story as breaking (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`):
- C6: "tanggap muna tayo ng **kapapasok lang na balita**" + reporter opens with "**Eksklusibo!**"
- C7: "**mainit-init na balita!** Kulong ang suspect… Nasa linya si Anne Poligrates"
- C1: "**Naku po! Mainit sa mata ng pulisya ngayon ang mga scammers**"
- C16 local: "**Eksklusibong** nakatutok d'yan si Joshua Casilla!"

`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN` (4 entries) — the "breaking" treatment is purely rhetorical: recency vocabulary ("kapapasok lang", "mainit-init"), the "Eksklusibo" flourish, and a live-phone SFX patch. None of these stories differ in structure from the standard local report. `ANALYST INFERENCE` — this is a safe energy boost: it costs one phrase and adds urgency, but only works if the story genuinely is fresh (all four are same-day arrests, so the claim holds).

**Lead verdict (`ANALYST INFERENCE`):** the strongest leads in the corpus are **action-first with the actor attached to the verb** ("Nakumpiska ng mga sundalong Pilipino…", "Timbog sa ikinasang operasyon ng Ormoc City Cyber Response Team…") because they deliver who-did-what in the first three seconds. Data-first leads work for the oil story because the number *is* the news. Fragments and warning-first leads consistently produce the weakest openings — C5's WPS report and C11's national report give the listener no subject for several seconds.

## 5. Source Attribution Practice

`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN` — every entry attributes somewhere, but discipline varies wildly.

**Named agency attributions (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`):**
- "Ayon sa National Security Council" — C1, C6, C9, C12, C17 (5 entries; the corpus's signature attribution line)
- "Ayon sa Philippine Sports Commission" — C4 (sports); "minomonitor ng Philippine Sports Commission" — C14 (sports)
- "Ayon sa Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group" — C7 (embedded: "sinisiguro ng PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group…")
- "Ayon sa Department of Energy" — C5 (infomercial PSA)
- "Ayon sa DENR" — C17 (infomercial)
- "Ayon sa ulat" — C15 sports (vague)
- "Ayon sa ilang kritiko" — C11 showbiz (vague plural)

**Named-person attributions:** "Dagdag pa ni Cornelio Valencia" (C7), "ayon kay Juniper Malinao ng National Bureau of Investigation" (C8), "Ayon kay US President Donald Trump" (C14, C16), "pahayag ni US President Donald Trump" (C3), "tiniyak ni US President Donald Trump" (C17), "sinabi ni US President Donald Trump" (C15), "Inanunsyo ng ating bagong Pangulong Marcos Jr." (C4). Named-person sourcing is a minority practice (8 reports) but is the corpus's clearest marker of script maturity (`ANALYST INFERENCE`).

**Hedging particles** ("umano", "di-umano'y", "diumano", "daw") appear in C1, C8, C17, C18 — used correctly for allegations ("di-umano'y paglalason" C1; "pinaniniwalaang ibinuhos umano" C18; "nagbuhos umano ng cyanide" C17). C5's "itinuturing ito na isang sabotahe galing sa China" is the same allegation **without** hedging or attribution — the single worst sourcing lapse in the corpus (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`).

**Sourcing gaps (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`):**
- C5's two hard-news reports contain **zero** attributions ("Nakapagtala rin ng pagbaba sa European and Asian markets" — from whom?).
- C2's national report names no source and mangles the agency ("Inatasan ang National Security Council of Armed Forces of the Philippines").
- C12's world report ("Bumalo na sumigit isang daang dolyar…") is unattributed.
- C16's world lead ("Sumirit na sa 43%…") is unattributed (attribution only appears in the Q&A follow-up: "ayon kay US President Donald Trump").
- C1's local lead ("Timbog… ang isang scammer") attributes the agency but never a police statement.
- C4's "Bagsak ngayon ang ekonomiya… kundi sa buong mundo" is a sweeping claim with no source, and its "fuel excise tax cut" (see §8) appears in no other entry — plausibly invented (`ANALYST INFERENCE`).

**Best-practice model (`ANALYST INFERENCE`):** C8's national report is the corpus's only fully-sourced report — attribution opens it ("ayon kay Juniper Malinao ng NBI"), a second named source ("ayon kay Cornelio Valencia") answers the anchor's question, and every claim is traceable. C17's national report is the best *agency-only* model: "Ayon sa National Security Council o NSC" follows the lead, and the NSC→DFA protest consequence is explicitly tied to the NSC.

## 6. Detail Ordering & Compression

**Ordering (`CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN`):** the invariant sequence across all 41 reports is **event → identity → legal/technical frame → consequence → advisory**. Identity (alias, age) always follows the lead, never precedes it (C1, C4, C6, C8, C13, C16, C18). Statutes are always sentence 2–3, never the lead (C4, C9, C12, C13, C14, C16, C18). Public advisories ("mag-report", "maging wais sa online transactions" C12) are always the closing fact before the sign-off. `ANALYST INFERENCE` — this ordering matches radio psychology: hook first, verify second, protect the listener last.

**Compression techniques (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`):**
1. **One-sentence time-and-place packing:** "…itong Pebrero 18 sa Ormoc City, Leyte" (C18); "dito sa Ormoc City noong 18 noong Pebrero" (C2); "bandang alas 3 ng hapon dito sa Ormoc City, Leyte" (C7); "Kaninang 3:45 PM" (C13). Four entries give clock-time detail; the rest give date only.
2. **Statute compression into a clause:** "na seryosong paglabag sa Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012" (C16); "sa ilalim ng Republic Act o RA 12010 at Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012 sa ilalim ng RA 10175" (C13 — double statute in one breath).
3. **Apposition instead of second sentences:** "sampung bote ng cyanide **o nakalalasong kemikal**" (C17); "automated teller machine **o ATM**" (C18); "AFP **o** PCG" (C6).
4. **List compression:** "overinflation, housing affordability at economic stability" (C5, C15) — three consequences in one clause.

**Compression failures (`DIRECTLY OBSERVED`):** C1's local lead repeats the agency mid-sentence: "…itong Pebrero 18 sa Ormoc City Cyber Response Team, Ormoc City, Leyte" — a location where "Ormoc City" was needed, the team name was re-inserted (redundancy); C5's WPS report fragments one event into six 2–4 word sentences, *inflating* rather than compressing ("Sa paligid ng Ayungin Shoal." is a standalone fragment); C18's sports lead misassigns the subject ("Hindi natuloy ang Filipino tennis player… sa naudlot na ATP Challenger" — the *player* didn't continue, rather than the *event* was canceled).

## 11. Reusable News-Writing Frameworks (from evidence)

### FRAMEWORK 1 — Local Crime/Arrest (canonical; 14-entry evidence base)
```
WARNING HOOK (anchor, 1 line)      "Huwag tayong maloko agad…" (C4)
→ HANDOFF (name + live location)   "Live sa Ormoc City, si…" (C2)
→ ARREST LEAD (passive verb + agency + age + crime + date + city)
→ IDENTIFICATION (alias, age)
→ LEGAL FRAME (statute: RA 12010, Cybercrime Act)
→ STATUS + PUBLIC ADVISORY ("nasa kustodiya… i-report ang modus")
→ Q&A EXTENSION (1 anchor question: cases? suspect status? police action?)
→ SIGN-OFF (name + beat/station) → THANKS
```
Variation A — **figurative lead** (C15, C16: "Rehas", "Himas-rehas"): replace the plain verb with one vivid prison idiom, keep everything else. Variation B — **warning-first** (C9, C11, C14): swap slots 3 and 1; use only when the advisory outranks the arrest in news value. `ANALYST INFERENCE` — Variation A is the stronger choice: it keeps the hook first.

### FRAMEWORK 2 — National/Security Story (14-entry evidence base)
```
CONTEXT (1 clause: tension frame)
→ ACTION LEAD (seizure or order; actor attached to verb)
→ SOURCE ("Ayon sa NSC…" or named official)
→ CONFIRMATION (evidence: water tested positive)
→ CHARACTERIZATION (hedged: "maituturing umanong sabotahe")
→ RESPONSE (AFP/PCG patrols; NSC → DFA protest)
→ STATUS (China silence, if any)
→ SIGN-OFF (name + "Patroller"/station)
```
Optional depth layer (issue variant, C3/C8/C16): insert one context sentence — historical incident (C8), legal ruling (C16), or diplomatic status (C3) — between CONFIRMATION and RESPONSE.

### FRAMEWORK 3 — International/Economic Story (12-entry evidence base)
```
NUMBER LEAD (one figure, one unit, one verb)     "Sumirit na sa 43%…" (C16)
→ CAUSE (one clause: Middle East tension)
→ CONSEQUENCE (one clause: markets/inflation/housing)
→ OFFICIAL STATEMENT (attributed: Trump/Iran status)
→ SIGN-OFF
```
Politics variant: swap in **statement-first** ("Ayon kay US President Donald Trump…") when the negotiation news outranks the price data (C14, C15, C17). `ANALYST INFERENCE` — never mix units (barrel vs gallon) or two price systems in one report (C17's $70/gal vs $3.98/gal contradiction is the cautionary tale).

### FRAMEWORK 4 — Breaking Label (4-entry evidence base)
Recency vocabulary ("kapapasok lang", "mainit-init", "Eksklusibo!") + live-patch SFX + normal report skeleton. One phrase of energy, zero structural change. Safe to reuse; do not overuse ("Eksklusibo" twice in one broadcast would devalue the label).

### FRAMEWORK 5 — Sign-Off Families (32/34 reporter reports)
- Safe default: **name + station** ("[Name], ulat sa NSPC Patrol").
- Strong upgrade: **beat persona** ("Nakatutok sa balita, [name]!" / "Nagpapatrol, [name]").
- Branding fusion: **station tagline** ("…Tatak-totoo!" C17) — only where the tagline is the station's own (§12).
- Avoid: location-only sign-off without a name (C7); garbled or missing sign-offs (C11, C12, C14, C16-national).

## 12. KEEP / ADAPT / DO NOT COPY

### KEEP AS A PRINCIPLE
1. **Lead with the verb and the actor** ("Nakumpiska ng mga sundalong Pilipino…" C13/C17; "Timbog…" C1) — never start a hard-news report with a fragment (C5, C9, C11 fail exactly here).
2. **3-sentence report, 25–35 seconds** — the corpus's proven block size (median 3 sentences; 16 of 41 reports).
3. **Attribution in the second breath**: "Ayon sa National Security Council" after the lead (C1, C6, C17); named officials for color (C7, C8).
4. **One statute, one clause**: "…seryosong paglabag sa Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012" (C16).
5. **Close with the public advisory**, then sign off — the listener always gets a takeaway before the name (C1, C4, C8, C16).
6. **Anchor Q&A as the second act** (C1, C2, C7, C8, C13, C14, C16): one question, one answer, one "Maraming salamat".
7. **Hedge allegations** with "umano/diumano/daw" (C17, C18) — C5's unhedged "sabotahe" is the counterexample.
8. **A fact sheet with internally consistent numbers** — the corpus's contradictions (ages 20/29/binatilyo; $3.98 vs $70; fire vs flood) all trace to absent verification.

### ADAPT CAREFULLY
1. **Figurative leads** ("Himas-rehas", "Rehas") — memorable, but limit to one per broadcast and only for crime stories; becomes a gimmick if repeated.
2. **"Eksklusibo!" breaking flourish** (C6) — one use per broadcast maximum.
3. **Patriotic sign-off tags** ("Patrol ng Bayan", "para sa bayang sinilangan", "bumabalita para sa masa") — strong but must match the station's own voice; C15 and C13 each use one, not a pile.
4. **Warning-first local leads** (C9, C11, C14) — viable only when the advisory is genuinely the bigger news.

### DO NOT COPY (proprietary identities)
- Program titles and callsigns: NSPC Patrol, DZJV/DCJV/DZJB/PCJV/PCTV 91.26 variants, "NSPC Patrol Online", "Balita Laging Online".
- Station taglines fused into sign-offs: "Tatak-totoo!" (C17), "Patrol ng Bayan" (C13), "nagmamasid sa mga balitang napapanahon" (C10), "para sa bayang sinilangan" (C16), "sports authority" (C18), "Showbiz Patroller, Yul Gonzales"-type beat personas.
- Reporter signature lines: "The winner takes it all!" (C9), "Mare, alam niyo ba…" (C8), "From the floor to the set, hashtag MVP thing" (C15).
