# Specialist K — Pacing & Timing

**Scope:** All 18 National-level NSPC radio-broadcasting entries (`transcripts/Contestant-1 … 18/actual-script.md`). Report focuses on *when things happen*: seconds per segment, seconds-to-first-headline, seconds-to-first-news, speaker-turn frequency, audio-cue (stinger) spacing, reporter-block duration, advocacy/sports/showbiz/closing durations, and the news-vs-soft-segment share of airtime.

**Evidence discipline:** Every claim is labeled **DIRECTLY OBSERVED** (from the timestamped rundown tables and timestamped dialogue in the primary sources), **CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN** (observed in multiple entries, named), or **ANALYST INFERENCE** (my interpretation). Per `ANALYSIS-SPEC.md` Section 1 and 3-K, all timing figures are **ESTIMATES**: they are derived from human-transcribed audio timestamps (inherently ±1–2 s), segment boundaries are rounded to the nearest second, and some scripts are condensed (see caveats). I do not claim precision that the sources do not support.

**Caveats carried over from the corpus inventory:**
- Broadcast windows differ (02:38–03:12 start); all times below are **broadcast-relative** unless stated (window start = 0:00).
- `mic-check.md` content is **excluded**; only the official broadcast window is measured.
- Contestant-8's infomercial dialogue is summarized, not verbatim (inventory §5); its infomercial *duration* is from its rundown, but its *turn count* undercounts real speech.
- Music/SFX are documented cue *labels*, not recordings (inventory §5.6); "audio cue" counts = cue labels in the script.
- Contestant-3, 4, 11 contain `[unclear]`/`[possibly: …]` passages; timestamps around those passages remain usable for pacing.

## 1. Method

- **Segment durations:** taken from each `actual-script.md` rundown table where present (all 18 have one); cross-checked against the timestamped dialogue where the rundown and dialogue disagreed (dialogue timestamps win, marked ESTIMATE).
- **Speaker turns:** counted mechanically across all 18 files (one turn per printed speaker label/dialogue block, e.g., `**ANCHOR 1 (Ralph Corton):** …` = 1 turn), then sanity-checked against manual tallies. Files where anchors deliver multi-sentence paragraphs (C10, C13, C16, C17) legitimately have few, long turns; files with 2–4-second lines (C4, C6, C18) legitimately have many. **All turn counts are ESTIMATES.**
- **Audio cues:** counted mechanically as bracketed cue labels (`[AUDIO: …]`, `[SFX: …]`, `[STINGER]`, `[…BED…]`, `[BUMPER]`, etc.) in the verbatim dialogue section only (rundown-table cue columns excluded to avoid double-counting). Verified against manual tallies. **All cue counts are ESTIMATES.**
- **Hard vs soft share:** "hard news" = local/national/world/international news segments; "soft" = infomercial + sports + showbiz + closing; "openers" = OBB/station ID + greetings + headline blocks + break bumpers/teasers + re-entries. The split of boundary segments is a judgment call — **ANALYST INFERENCE** on top of DIRECTLY OBSERVED segment boundaries.

## 3. Segment-Level Duration Patterns

### 3.1 Opening phase: OBB / station ID / greeting / headlines

**DIRECTLY OBSERVED ranges (window-relative):**

- **OBB/station-ID block:** 17–54 s (C11 shortest 17 s; C15 longest 54 s — C15 opens with a vocal theme song). Typical: 20–40 s. 15/18 entries complete a station ID/jingle before any anchor greeting.
- **Anchor greeting + date/time:** 10–50 s, typically 15–40 s. All 18 include a greeting and most a date; 12/18 include an explicit time check (C1, C2, C3, C4, C6, C9, C11, C14, C15, C16, C18 — and C5's "12 pa lang ng hapon" banter). Time checks cluster either in the opening (C1, C6, C14, C16, C18) or right after the infomercial break (C2, C3, C9, C11, C15).
- **Headline block:** 10–32 s for the standalone blocks (C2 26 s, C5 26 s, C7 17 s, C9 14 s, C11 18 s, C13 ~32 s, C14 30 s, C15 10 s, C16 ~12 s, C17 16 s); 2–4 headlines per block, each 4–10 s with a stinger between (e.g., C4's four headlines at 04:02–04:15 with `[HEADLINE STINGER 1–4]`; C1's two headline teases at 03:22–03:50).

**Two opening architectures (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN):**
1. **Teaser-first** (headlines before greetings): C2, C8, C10, C11, C13 — first headline within ~0–21 s. C2 opens directly with the headline rundown ("Ang insidente ng panlalason…"), C13 opens with "Heto na mga balita", C8 embeds headlines in the OBB.
2. **Greeting-first** (greeting/date/branding before any headline): C1, C3, C4, C5, C6, C7, C9, C12, C14, C15, C16, C17, C18 — first headline at ~33–69 s.

**Seconds-to-first-headline:** 0–69 s; mean ≈ 35 s, median ≈ 39 s (ESTIMATES).
**Seconds-to-first-news (first hard-news report begins):** 52–88 s; mean ≈ 66 s, median ≈ 64 s (ESTIMATES). C16 is slowest (88 s), C17 fastest (52 s).

### 3.2 News spine

**DIRECTLY OBSERVED:** every entry carries 2–4 hard-news reports between the opening and the break; report blocks (anchor lead-in + reporter + return/thank-you) run 26–56 s, with 30–50 s typical. Examples: C1 local 46 s (incl. a Q&A exchange), C1 national 48 s; C2 national 29 s, local 45 s; C4 national 53 s, local 45 s; C7 local 41 s, national 43 s; C8 local 49 s, national 54 s; C13 local 56 s (longest single block, incl. live Q&A); C5 global 26 s (shortest block).

**Reporter solo speech** within a block is ~20–35 s; the remaining ~10–20 s is anchor lead-in (5–15 s), stinger/bed transition (1–3 s), and anchor thank-you/return (2–5 s). Example (DIRECTLY OBSERVED, C1): lead-in 03:51–03:59 (8 s) → reporter 04:00–04:36 (36 s incl. Q&A) → thank-you 04:36–04:37 (1 s).

**Anchor→reporter handoff pattern (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN, 15/18 entries):** anchor names the reporter and topic (5–15 s), stinger/bed change, reporter delivers 20–35 s, sign-off line with name, anchor thank-you. Seven entries include live-Q&A cross-talk in the local report (C1, C2, C4, C7, C8, C13, C14), which adds ~8–15 s to that block (ANALYST INFERENCE: the Q&A is the pacing tool that prevents back-to-back monologues).

### 3.3 Break, infomercial, re-entry

**DIRECTLY OBSERVED:**
- **Break teaser/bumper before the infomercial:** 5–16 s, mean ≈ 9 s (C1 16 s, C2 10 s, C4 9 s, C6 8 s, C7 4 s, C8 ~5 s, C9 6 s, C10 6 s, C11 4 s, C13 6 s, C14 10 s, C15 6 s, C16 13 s, C17 10 s, C18 13 s). C3, C5, C12 go straight into the infomercial with only a stinger (no spoken teaser).
- **Infomercial/advocacy:** 35–69 s; mean ≈ 49 s, median ≈ 48 s (C7 64 s and C8 69 s are the outliers; C2 35 s shortest).
- **Break position:** infomercial starts at broadcast-relative 2:02–3:04, mean ≈ 2:35 — i.e., the commercial break sits just past the middle of the ~5-minute broadcast.
- **Re-entry after break:** 3–10 s; either a station-ID stinger + "nagbabalik ang NSPC Patrol" line (10/18), or a time check + return (C2, C3, C9, C11, C15).

### 3.4 Sports and showbiz

**DIRECTLY OBSERVED:**
- **Sports (14/18 entries):** 30–47 s, mean ≈ 37 s. Anchor lead-in 5–12 s, sportscaster 20–35 s.
- **Showbiz (17/18):** 16–49 s, mean ≈ 36 s. C4 is the outlier (16 s, co-anchor-read); C8 (49 s) and C10 (48 s) longest.
- **Order:** when both present, showbiz follows sports in 10 of 14 entries (C3 and C11 reverse it: showbiz before sports); in 3 entries (C6, C12, C16) sports is absent and showbiz closes the entertainment stretch; C7 has neither sports nor showbiz after the international report — it goes straight from international news to closing editorial (DIRECTLY OBSERVED, and an unusual pacing choice: a 64 s infomercial + no soft segment after it).
- Sports starts at broadcast-relative 3:06–4:15 (mean ≈ 3:37); showbiz starts at 2:58–4:34 (mean ≈ 3:56). Both are **last-third** content.

### 3.5 Closing

**DIRECTLY OBSERVED:** 12–33 s; mean ≈ 20 s, median ≈ 19 s. Two-step structure: (1) recap/editorial line by the anchors (3–10 s), (2) anchor IDs + station ID/slogan + ensemble sign-off + outro swell (10–25 s). C17's closing is the longest (33 s — a four-line editorial before the IDs); C13's is the shortest (12 s, one recap line + "LABAN, PILIPINAS!").

### 3.6 News vs soft-segment share (ESTIMATES, ANALYST INFERENCE for the split)

| Band | Hard news | Soft (advocacy+sports+showbiz+closing) | Openers/transitions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Range (18 entries) | 23–41 % | 29–54 % | 17–32 % |
| Mean | ~30 % | ~44 % | ~25 % |

**ANALYST INFERENCE:** on average less than a third of the airtime is hard news; the "news program" is really a variety program. This is consistent with the competition format requiring multiple demonstrated genres in ~5 minutes, and it is the single biggest structural constraint on pacing: each genre gets one 30–50 s slot and must be entered and exited quickly.

## 4. Speaker-Turn and Audio-Cue Rhythm (ESTIMATES)

### 4.1 Turn frequency

| Metric | Range | Median |
|---|---|---|
| Turns per entry | 22 (C10) – 186 (C6) | ~56 |
| Avg turn length | 1.6 s (C6) – 13.3 s (C10) | ~4.5 s |
| Turns per minute | 4.5 (C10) – 38.2 (C6) | ~13 |

**Three delivery styles (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN):**
1. **Ultra-fragmented** (avg turn ≤ 3 s): C2 (3.0 s), C4 (2.7 s), C6 (1.6 s), C18 (1.7 s). Lines of 1–3 words per timestamp; e.g., C18's sportscaster delivers "Hindi natuloy / ang Filipino tennis player / na si Francis Casey Alcantara" as three separate turns; C6's newscaster delivers "Nagpapatrol, / Lianne Sumagaysay" as two. This produces a machine-gun rhythm but risks sounding breathless (ANALYST INFERENCE; see weaknesses).
2. **Balanced newsroom** (avg turn 3–8 s): C1 (4.6 s), C3 (4.1 s), C5 (4.3 s), C9 (3.4 s), C11 (5.5 s), C14 (6.3 s), C15 (4.5 s). Alternation of 2–4 s anchor lines and 5–10 s reporter sentences.
3. **Block style** (avg turn 7–14 s): C7 (7.6 s), C8 (6.8 s), C10 (13.3 s), C12 (6.8 s), C13 (8.1 s), C16 (8.4 s), C17 (8.1 s). Anchors and reporters deliver full paragraphs; C10's anchors read 30–40 s monologue blocks (e.g., the national-news segment 03:58–04:36 is one 38 s turn).

**Anchor-pair alternation is the engine of opening/headline pace** (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN): in the headline/greeting phase, turns change every 2–5 s (C1: ~11 turns in 03:20–03:51, ≈2.8 s/turn; C6: ~22 turns in 03:29–04:14, ≈2.0 s/turn). Both anchors exchange single sentences or sentence fragments ("…at busy sa gawaing bahay…" / "…tumutok na!" — C1 03:43–03:45). This is where the scripts feel fastest even in slow-opening entries.

### 4.2 Audio-cue (stinger) spacing

| Metric | Range | Median |
|---|---|---|
| Cues per entry | 13 (C1, C16) – 28 (C5) | ~20 |
| Cues per minute | 2.4 (C16) – 5.5 (C5) | ~3.9 |
| Seconds between cues | ~11 s (C5) – ~25 s (C16) | ~15 s |

**CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN:** a documented audio cue lands roughly every 12–25 seconds, i.e., **about one cue per 2–4 dialogue turns**. Density is not uniform within a broadcast:
- **Dense zones** (a cue every 3–10 s): openings/OBBs (stingers, drum accents), headline blocks (one stinger per headline), segment transitions, and SFX-laden infomercial drama scenes (C3's AI-drama packs 7 cues into 46 s — keyboard typing, robotic chime, thunder drop, evil laugh; C2's classroom skit runs 3 cues into 19 s).
- **Sparse zones** (no cues for 20–45 s): reporter monologues inside a news bed (e.g., C1 national report 04:51–05:22 has no cue; C5 global report 04:22–04:46 none), and C10's/C16's long anchor monologues.

**ANALYST INFERENCE on function:** stingers act as *attention resets* at exactly the points where the listener's attention must be re-seized (new headline, new category, return from break) and are deliberately withheld during sustained speech. The observed median (~1 cue/15 s) is the "resting" reset rate; the opening phases run 2–3× hotter.

### 4.3 Combined attention-reset rate

Turns + cues per minute (my combined proxy for "attention resets per minute", ESTIMATE): range ~8 (C10) to ~42 (C6); median ≈ 14. The four ultra-fragmented entries (C2, C4, C6, C18) run 20–42 resets/min; the four block-style entries (C10, C13, C16, C17) run 8–12 resets/min.

## 5. Common Pacing Patterns (summary)

1. **Opening ≈ 55–90 s before the first news story** (mean 66 s; range 52–88 s) — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN (18/18). The first minute is branding + greeting + 2–4 headlines, not news.
2. **Two opening architectures:** teaser-first (C2, C8, C10, C11, C13) vs greeting-first (13 entries) — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN.
3. **Headlines are stingered, one per headline** — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN (all entries that have distinct headline blocks; C1, C4, C5, C7, C9, C10, C12, C13, C14, C16, C18 show per-headline stingers).
4. **2–4 news reports of ~30–55 s each** before the break — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN.
5. **The commercial break lands at ~2:00–3:00** of a ~5:00 broadcast (mean 2:35) — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN (18/18).
6. **Advocacy ≈ 40–55 s** (mean 49 s) with a drama-hook first half and a narrated message second half — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN (18/18). Outliers C7/C8 (64/69 s).
7. **Sports ≈ 30–47 s, showbiz ≈ 30–49 s**, both in the last third; showbiz after sports when both exist (10/14) — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN.
8. **Closing ≈ 15–25 s** (mean 20 s), two-step: recap/editorial → IDs + station slogan + ensemble sign-off — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN (18/18).
9. **Anchor-pair alternation every 2–5 s during openers**; reporter monologues 20–35 s uninterrupted — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN.
10. **An audio cue roughly every 15 s on average**, clustered at boundaries, withheld during monologues — CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN (see §4.2).

## 6. Typical National-Level Pacing Profile (composite; all values ESTIMATES)

A single "average broadcast" assembled from the 18 entries (broadcast-relative clock, ~5:00 total):

| Clock | Phase | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | OBB / station ID / jingle | 20–40 s | chorus or VO; slogan + callsign; 2–4 stingers |
| 0:30–1:00 | Greeting, date/time, anchor IDs | 15–40 s | rapid anchor alternation |
| 0:40–1:10 | Headline block | 10–30 s | 2–4 headlines, one stinger each |
| ~1:05 | **First news story begins** | — | mean 66 s; range 52–88 s |
| 1:05–2:30 | News spine | ~85 s | 2–4 reports, 30–55 s each; anchor lead-in 5–15 s, reporter 20–35 s, thank-you 2–5 s |
| 2:20–2:45 | Break teaser/bumper | 5–16 s | tease next category ("pagbabalik") |
| 2:35–3:25 | Infomercial/advocacy | ~49 s | hook/drama 10–20 s → narrated message + slogan 20–35 s |
| 3:25–3:35 | Re-entry + (optional) time check | 3–10 s | station ID stinger |
| 3:35–4:15 | Sports (if present) | ~37 s | anchor lead-in 5–12 s, sportscaster 20–35 s |
| 4:00–4:40 | Showbiz (if present) | ~36 s | lighter bed, reporter 25–40 s |
| 4:35–5:00 | Closing | ~20 s | recap → anchor IDs → station ID/slogan → ensemble sign-off → outro swell |

**Structural ratios of the typical broadcast:** ~25% openers/transitions, ~30% hard news, ~45% soft segments (advocacy + sports + showbiz + closing). **Rhythm:** ~13 turns/min and ~1 cue/15 s at rest; 2–3× hotter during openers and transitions; reporter blocks are the "slow" islands of sustained speech.

**ANALYST INFERENCE — why this profile works:** the format demands (a) prove the newsroom in the first minute, (b) demonstrate the advocacy genre mid-show, (c) finish with the two light genres and a clean sign-off. The observed timing is what that constraint produces: every genre transition is a reset point, so the scripts concentrate their stingers there rather than inside reports.

## 7. Notable Exceptions and Pacing Risks

**Exceptions (DIRECTLY OBSERVED):**
- **C6, C12, C13, C16: no sports segment** — C6 and C16 substitute an international news report; C12 and C13 end their entertainment stretch with showbiz only.
- **C7: no showbiz segment** — goes international news → 64 s infomercial → sports → closing editorial.
- **C4: showbiz = 16 s**, read by the co-anchor; C4 also has the longest greeting phase before headlines (~32 s of greetings before the first headline at 04:02).
- **C16: slowest first news (88 s)** — time check + OBB + long double greeting.
- **C5: slowest first news among the teaser-less (85 s)** — its OBB is a sung theme; it also carries the densest cue schedule (5.5 cues/min).
- **C12: break structure is inverted** — the "Malami ng pagka-aresto…" teaser appears *inside* the infomercial segment window (05:40–06:23), an unusual seam.
- **C13: fastest headline entry (0–21 s)** and shortest closing (12 s).

**Pacing risks (ANALYST INFERENCE, from observed patterns):**
- **Ultra-fragmented delivery (C2, C4, C6, C18; avg turn ≤ 3 s)** risks breathlessness and listener fatigue; combined with slips (`[unclear]` tags concentrate in these entries) the reset rate (~20–42/min) may outrun comprehensibility. The block-style entries (C10, C16, C17) have the opposite risk: 30–40 s monologues with no reset can lose a listener — C10's 38 s single-turn national report is the extreme.
- **Long openings** (C16 88 s, C5 85 s, C13 81 s to first news) spend ~a third of the broadcast before any hard news — a structural risk if the greeting phase is not entertaining.
- **C7's 64 s advocacy + C8's 69 s advocacy** consume ~20% of the broadcast in one segment, leaving compressed closing/soft segments.
- **C4's 16 s showbiz** gives the lightest genre the least airtime — a genre-coverage imbalance.

## 8. Key Takeaways for Script Revision

1. First news at ~60–70 s is the National norm; aim for ~55–65 s (C1, C3, C7, C8, C11, C17, C18 cluster at 52–62 s). **(CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)**
2. Deliver 2–4 reports of 30–50 s each; keep reporter solo speech ≤ ~35 s; use one live-Q&A exchange to break up the news spine. **(CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)**
3. Place the advocacy at ~2:20–2:45 of a 5:00 show and hold it to 40–55 s. **(CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)**
4. Budget ~30–45 s for sports and ~30–40 s for showbiz in the last third, and 15–25 s for the closing. **(CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)**
5. Concentrate stingers at genre boundaries and per-headline; withhold cues inside reporter monologues. **(CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN; supported by 18/18 entries' cue placement)**
6. Keep the headline/greeting phase in rapid anchor alternation (2–5 s per turn) — it is the fastest-feeling part of every entry. **(CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)**
7. Avoid both extremes: sub-3 s average turns and 30+ s monologues both appeared at National level and both carry comprehensibility/listener-retention risk. **(ANALYST INFERENCE)**
