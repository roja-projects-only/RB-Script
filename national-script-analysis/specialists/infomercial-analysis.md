## 4. Common Framework — derived with evidence

Across 17 intelligible entries (C8 excluded), the segment follows a consistent skeleton. The canonical formula:

**ENTRY BUMPER (return promise + tease) → HOOK → CHARACTER/SCENARIO → PROBLEM/CONFLICT → INFORMATION → SOLUTION → CALL TO ACTION → SLOGAN/TAGLINE → RETURN STINGER**

Each step is evidenced below.

### Step 1 — ENTRY BUMPER: "We will return" + teaser
- "Huwag kayong aalis, kami ay magbabalik!" (C7); "Manatiling nakatutok at kami'y magbabalik pagkatapos ng isang paalala" (C10); "Magbabalik kami pagkatapos ng isang paalala" (C11); "coffee break muna tayo dahil magbabalik pa… ang NSPC Patrol!" (C1); "Huwag mag-lag, magbabalik ang NSPC Patrol Online" (C8). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- 16/18 entries use explicit return-promise language; the other two (C3, C12) cut straight to the break bed. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN: C1, C2, C4, C5, C6, C7, C8, C9, C10, C11, C13, C14, C15, C16, C17, C18)
- Half of the bumpers also *tease what's coming after the break* (C1 sports+Oscars, C2 showbiz, C4 sports, C6 Oscars, C9 sports+showbiz, C13 Oscars, C16 film awards, C14 "iba pang mga balita"). **Function (ANALYST INFERENCE):** the promise converts the break from a dead stop into a held thread — the listener stays to collect the payoff.
- C5 is the thematic outlier: its bumper continues the program's road-trip metaphor ("red light muna") instead of announcing a break. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)

### Step 2 — HOOK: sound + first line, ~5 seconds
The hook is always either a **sound** (school bell C2, keyboard+panic C3, traffic C5, game-show jingle C1/C18), a **startling line** ("Ma, mukhang di ako makakagraduate, Ma." C4; "Nag-iiwanan ako!" C14; "Wala na akong pera!" C5), or a **question** ("Alam niyo ba na ayon sa batas…?" C7; "ano ang ibig sabihin ng acronym na DEPED?" C18). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED, all)
- The strongest hooks create a mini-mystery: C1's guessing game and C18's quiz both withhold the topic for 10–15 seconds. (ANALYST INFERENCE)

### Step 3 — CHARACTER/SCENARIO: dramatized 3-voice dialogue
- 17/18 entries use characters; the typical cast is **3 voices** (C2, C3, C4, C6, C7, C11, C13, C14, C15, C17 — 10 entries); ensemble entries use 4–6 (C1, C5, C9, C10, C16, C18). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED; count is a CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)
- Recurring character pairs: **adult authority + struggling child** (Teacher/Student C2, C6; Mother/Child C4, C11, C13; Store Owner/Child C15; Mentor/Student C14) — the "expert/layperson" dialogue scaffold. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN: C2, C4, C6, C11, C13, C14, C15)
- Only C7, C10, C17 use direct-address advocacy (no scene) — C7's call-and-response and chant are the *voice-heavy* alternative. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)

### Step 4 — PROBLEM/CONFLICT: personal stakes first
- The problem is always dramatized before it is stated abstractly: C2's "Wala."; C4's "Mababagsak na ako sa English!"; C5's "Wala na akong pera! Anong gagawin ko?"; C13's "Two years ka na sa grade 5 ba?"; C14's "Paano na ang mga pangarap ko?" (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- Two entries escalate to **confrontation**: C3 (AI threatens humanity) and C10 (neighbor threatens the dog). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)

### Step 5 — INFORMATION: the weakest universal step
- ~10/18 entries attempt a fact: statistics in C1 ("7 sa 10"), C2 ("3 sa 10" — mangled), C4 ("1%" — mangled/inverted), C13 ("30 to 40 percent"), C5 (DOE price), C7 (missing number), C10 (RA 8485), C18 (trimester mechanics), C17 (DENR causes), C6 ("foundational skills" claim). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- C9, C11, C14, C15, C16 and C3 carry **no statistic at all**. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- Source attribution appears in C5 (Department of Energy), C10 (RA 8485), C13 (pag-aaral), C17 (DENR), C18 (DepEd) — only 5/18 name a source. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN: C5, C10, C13, C17, C18)

### Step 6 — SOLUTION
- Solution forms: (a) **named program** (C13 ARAL, C16 ALS/Open High School/FSL, C18 trimester, C15 "ipon sa bangko"); (b) **behavior change** (C1 training + materials, C5 pagtitipid, C11 MMR, C7 proper care); (c) **abstraction** (C3 "mas makapangyarihan ang may tunay na karunungan", C14 "edukasyon ang susi", C6 "mag-aral nang mabuti"). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- **The named-program solution is the strongest form** because the listener leaves with something searchable (ANALYST INFERENCE): C13 is the only entry where the statistic and the solution are the *same object* (ARAL improved proficiency → support ARAL).

### Step 7 — CALL TO ACTION: the most inconsistent step

## 5. Sound-Design Patterns

- **Every entry marks the break with a dedicated bed change** — `[INFOMERCIAL BED]`, `[DRAMATIC BED]`, `[ADVOCACY BED]`, `[GAME SHOW BED]`, `[INFOMERCIAL JINGLE]` — distinct from the news beds. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN, 18/18 DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- **Genre-matched ambience** is the differentiator: classroom bell (C2), keyboard + robotic filter + thunder + evil laugh (C3), dramatic piano swelling to strings (C4), vehicle engine/traffic (C5), game-show uptempo (C1, C18), chant jingle (C7, C17). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- **The break is bracketed by stingers**: entry (commercial-bumper stinger) and exit (return stinger). Only C3 and C12 lack a documented exit stinger. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)
- **Music carries the emotional pivot**: C4 and C13 script a *swell* at the moment the drama hands off to the narrator ("MUSIC SWELLS TO INSPIRING STRINGS" C4; "ADVOCACY MUSIC BED SWELLS" C13) — the standard radio PSA trick of scoring the tonal shift (ANALYST INFERENCE).
- **Caveat (from corpus inventory §5):** music/SFX entries are cue labels, not recordings; actual bed styles cannot be verified from text.

## 7. Critical Assessment

### Weak / missing calls to action (9 of 18)
C3, C4, C6, C8, C12, C16, C18 contain no actionable instruction at all; C2, C9, C11, C14, C17 substitute sentiments ("Boses mo ang instrumento sa pagbabago ng lipunan", "dapat silang suportahan at gabayan"). **Analyst note (ANALYST INFERENCE):** a PSA without a "do X" instruction is a mood piece — memorable but inert. The two strongest CTAs in the corpus (C7 "I-report ang mga paglabag sa Animal Welfare Act!", C13 "suportahan ang DepEd ARAL Program") both name an *entity*; that is the transferable principle.

### Logic gaps
- **C3:** the segment is titled "Responsible Use of AI" but the copy argues *against* AI ("mas makapangyarihan ang may tunay na karunungan") — the stated topic and the argument never meet; no responsible-use guidance is given. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED; contradiction is ANALYST INFERENCE)
- **C4:** the statistic is delivered inverted ("1%" for "high percentage"), which if heard literally says the problem barely exists. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- **C7:** the statistic without a number ("[porsyento] ng mga pamilyang Pilipino ay may alagang hayop") breaks the argument the chant built. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- **C14:** "Nag-iiwanan ako" is delivered as an ungrammatical open; the rhyming motto compensates but the message is vague. (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)
- **C8/C12:** as documented, the segments cannot be assessed — for C12 this is likely a production/transcription failure; either way, no evaluable content exists in the primary source. (ANALYST INFERENCE)

### Taste risks
- **C10's dog-eating joke** ("parang masarap din 'yang kainin!") — memorable dark humor, but animal cruelty is a sensitive topic; a competition jury may read it as flippant rather than impactful. (ANALYST INFERENCE)
- **C3's AI-as-villain** (robotic voice + "Kayo ang may gawa nito… kami na ang masusunod" + evil laugh) — melodrama that risks feeling like a skit rather than an advocacy; also tech-pessimism that contradicts the "responsible use" brief. (ANALYST INFERENCE)
- **Shaming frames of children:** C2 ("Wala." — students refuse to try), C6 ("Mahina si Ruben"), C13 ("Ang tanda mo na? Hindi ka pa rin magaling magbasa?") place the failure on the child rather than the system — risky in an education advocacy whose stated villain is the system. (ANALYST INFERENCE)
- **C5's "nag-relapse ka na"** uses a clinical term (relapse) as slang for exhaustion, immediately after the break — a light-usage accuracy concern. (ANALYST INFERENCE)
- **C18's comic wrong answers** are safely self-deprecating (the *contestants* are wrong, not the audience), and the host corrects them — good handling of comedy in advocacy (ANALYST INFERENCE).

### Integration into the program
- Best-integrated: C5 (thematic continuity with the road-trip concept), C1 and C18 (program-energy formats — quiz/game show — that match the station's playful anchors), C13/C7 (teased and returned with full station identity). (ANALYST INFERENCE)
- Least integrated: C14 (break ends on the PSA motto; no verbal return), C12 (no return mechanism at all), C16 (the bumper teases *showbiz*, so the PSA arrives unannounced). (DIRECTLY OBSERVED)

## 8. Reusable Principles (for the future Division-level script)

1. **Promise the return, then deliver it with a station stinger.** The "Huwag kayong aalis, kami ay magbabalik" + re-entry ID bracket is the corpus's most reliable break architecture. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)
2. **Hook with a sound and a question, not a greeting.** The best openings (C1 quiz, C2 "Wala.", C18 quiz) create 5–10 seconds of mystery before the topic is named. (ANALYST INFERENCE)
3. **Three-voice dialogue (authority + child + narrator) is the efficient default** — enough texture for a scene, cheap enough for 40–50 seconds. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)
4. **Chain fact → named solution → named CTA.** C13 is the model: statistic, program, and instruction in one sentence. Avoid the corpus's most common failure: a statistic with no source, or an advocacy with no action. (ANALYST INFERENCE)
5. **Close with a custom slogan or the station-attribution tag** — the tag ("Ang paalalang ito ay hatid sa inyo ng himpilang ito") is a safe, conventional closer used by 5 entries. (CROSS-SCRIPT PATTERN)
6. **Avoid shaming frames and inverted stats** — the two most damaging defects observed were the "1%" inversion (C4) and child-blaming dialogue (C2, C6, C13). (ANALYST INFERENCE)
7. **Keep the break between 40–50 seconds** (mean ≈ 46s); C7/C8 at 64–65s show the ceiling where a mid-broadcast PSA starts crowding the news. (ANALYST INFERENCE from duration data)
