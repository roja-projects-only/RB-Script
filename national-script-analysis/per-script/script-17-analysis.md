- Xianne (attribution + key detail): `"Ayon sa National Security Council o NSC, kinumpirmang positibo sa cyanide ang tubig sa paligid ng Ayungin Shoal at maituturing umanong pansasabotahe ng China sa supply ng pagkain ng mga sundalo ng Hukbong Dagat."` — source-first (`Ayon sa NSC`) → confirmation → motive attribution (sabotage).
- Ivan (consequence): `"Sa kasalukuyan, plano nang magsumite ng ulat ang NSC sa Department of Foreign Affairs bilang basehan para sa diplomatikong protesta ng bansa kontra China."` — plan → purpose (diplomatic protest).
- Facts packed: quantity (10 bottles), actors (AFP soldiers, Chinese fishing vessels), place (WPS, Ayungin Shoal), hazard, source (NSC), motive (sabotage), next step (DFA protest). ~8 facts in 4 sentences. DIRECTLY OBSERVED.

**Story B — US crude oil surge (World/Economic, 04:22–05:01).**
- Xianne lead-in (2 sentences): `"Samantala, pumalo sa halos anim na porsyento (6%) ang itinaas sa presyo ng crude oil sa Estados Unidos sa loob ng isang araw bunsod dito sa nangyayaring sigalot sa Gitnang Silangan."` + handoff.

- Pattern: LEAD (price) → SUPPORTING DATA (gasoline) → RESULT (6%) → CONTEXT/CONCERN (inflation) → AUTHORITY ASSURANCE (Trump) → SIGN-OFF. DIRECTLY OBSERVED. Note: the 6% fact appears in both the lead-in and the report — reinforcement, but also duplication (Section 24).

**Underlying pattern for hard news (A + B):** LEAD/CONTEXT → EVENT → SOURCE ATTRIBUTION → KEY DETAIL → CONSEQUENCE/RESPONSE → SIGN-OFF, with attribution placed mid-story (`Ayon sa NSC…`) rather than at the lead. ANALYST INFERENCE, derived from this script's evidence.

The system's weakness: the repeated bumper pattern (`Mas marami pang mga balita sa pagbabalik…` / `Tuloy-tuloy pa rin ang balitaan…`) is formulaic — effective, but a third identical pattern would have become robotic. ### 25. Reusable Lessons

## KEEP AS A PRINCIPLE

- **Question handoffs to reporters** ("Zal Ramos, ano ang latest?") — convert a transition into anticipation; reword for your own show.
- **The "X o Y" gloss frame** ("inflation o bilis ng pagtaas sa presyo ng mga bilihin") — translate English terms in-line for listeners without pausing the sentence.
- **Return-promise bumpers around inserted content** ("Mas marami pang mga balita sa pagbabalik…") — tell the listener the news will resume; it makes a PSA feel like a scheduled break, not an interruption.
- **Bilingual bookends** — open and close in the local language (here Bisaya) to claim a hometown identity at the cheapest cost: two lines.
- **Close with place + purpose** ("Mula sa silong ng Green Pineapple of the Philippines, patuloy ang pagmamasid sa katotohanan") — fuse the locality tagline with a mission statement and echo the opening slogan.
- **Ensemble call-and-response finish** ("Ito ang… / NSPC Patrol! / Magandang araw, Pilipinas!") — let the whole team sign off; a unison close is a team signature no solo anchor can produce.
- **Mirror-image closings** — echo the open (greeting, self-IDs, station name, music) in reverse order at the end; completion comes from symmetry.
- **One bed per category, stingers at every boundary, beds ducked "up and under"** — listeners should know the genre from sound alone.
- **A punchy launch line after headlines** ("Simulan na ang balitaan!") — one imperative converts teases into a start signal.
- **Connect stories by shared context** (Middle East conflict in world news AND sports) — separate reports begin to feel like one day's news.

## ADAPT CAREFULLY

- **Slogan-family wordplay** (Tatak-totoo / Tatak-tutok / Tatak-patrol): the idea of one stem inflected per genre is strong, but every variant must be deliberate and consistent — an unexplained drift reads as an error. If you adapt it, fix the rules first.
- **Direct-address tease naming a cast member** ("Roy Quemado, handa ka na…"): works only when the PSA is a real drama with named characters, and the tease must be comprehensible (this one isn't). Reword before reuse.
- **Reporter persona tags** ("Ako ang inyong standby sa chikahan"): segment-specific reporter identities add color, but the tag should be earned by the report's tone — a persona line on a colorless report feels hollow.
- **Per-segment music beds:** excellent clarity but demanding on the board operator (17 cues in 5 minutes here). A division-level team with one operator may prefer 2–3 beds + stingers; the clarity goal, not the file count, is the principle.
- **Hometown motifs ("silong")**: charming when bookended, but only if the meaning is transparent to your actual audience and the station actually carries local content — this one doesn't, and the mismatch shows.
- **Bisaya/Tagalog mixing:** only where the listening market genuinely speaks it; in a national competition it reads as regional identity, which was this team's intent.

## DO NOT COPY

- "NSPC Patrol" (program name) and the "Radyo Patrol" sub-brand.
- "Tatak-totoo!" and all its variants ("Tatak-tutok!", "Tatak-patrol") — the slogan family is this team's identity.
- "Mula sa sulok ng bansa hanggang sa bawat dako ng mundo, para sa sambayanang Pilipino." — the ensemble ID line.
- "Mula sa silong ng Green Pineapple of the Philippines, patuloy ang pagmamasid sa katotohanan." — the locality-mission closing line.
- "Ako ang inyong standby sa chikahan, Zal Ramos, Tatak-totoo!" — the persona sign-off.
- "Magandang araw, Pilipinas!" as a final unison line, and the "Ito ang… [NAME]!" call-and-response pairing as a closing device.
- "Simulan na ang balitaan!" / "Tuloy-tuloy pa rin ang balitaan dito sa NSPC Patrol!" / "Mas marami pang mga balita sa pagbabalik ng NSPC Patrol!" — the bumper catchphrases.
- The advocacy jingle's lyrics ("Ating alagaan ang kalikasan upang mga hayop ating matulungan…") — if you write a PSA song, write your own.
