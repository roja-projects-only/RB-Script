### 25. Reusable Lessons

## KEEP AS A PRINCIPLE

- **Assign teaser headlines to the reporters who will cover them** — the OBB becomes a cast preview and a story-order map in one pass (Contestant-2 OBB [03:12–03:38]). Principle: voice-match the tease.
- **Let the anchor re-enter a live report with one question** — a single scripted question mid-report doubles the reporter's block and creates a genuine two-way exchange ("Mica, ano naman ang naging babalak na pulisya umpun dito?").
- **Hand the story's consequence to the co-anchor** — "Dahil dito, Ella." keeps the duo narratively engaged and cleanly splits long news items (lead/context vs. consequence/resolution).
- **Give each category its own music bed and its own reporter sign-off formula** — the listener should be able to identify the beat by sound before a word is spoken; sign-offs should follow one formula: "Ako si [NAME] + [category tag]."
- **Punctuate every headline with a distinct stinger** — drum/brass/swoosh separators made the OBB legible even when lines were garbled.
- **Re-anchor after the commercial break with a package** — station ID stinger → chime → spoken time check (06:14–06:20) instantly restores program identity.
- **Build a PSA as joke-then-fact** — a comedic micro-scene earns attention for the statistic that follows; keep the scene shorter than the facts (~1:2 or tighter).
- **Notate music levels in the script** ("UP AND UNDER", swell/dip/stop) — it tells the board op exactly what to do and keeps dialogue audible.

## ADAPT CAREFULLY

- **The "live phone-patch field report" convention** — effective (and the Q&A variant is worth keeping), but it is a stock NSPC device; overuse across our scripts will read as formula. Use for ONE local story per broadcast, never more.
- **The PSA classroom drama** — the "teacher asks, class answers Wala" beat is charming, but the teacher/student PSA is the most common advocacy frame in this corpus; our version needs a different scene (workplace, home, street) or a different comedic engine.
- **English tagline sprinkling** ("all-in sa Balitang Trending") — fun, but only works if the rest of the segment is pronounceable; adapt the *structure* (personal catchphrase on the sign-off), not the English-verb pattern.
- **The "So yan ang iba pa sa pagbabalik..." break bumper formula** — the *intent* (promise more after the break) is right; write it as a complete sentence and verify it verbally before air.
- **Tagline pairs ("naninindigan para sa katotohanan" + "serbisyo at malasakit")** — a two-line values statement at the intro is a good idea; ensure it is pronounceable in one breath by both anchors and repeat it at the close for the bookend.

## DO NOT COPY

- **"NSPC Patrol" / "NSBC Patrol"** as a program title — it is the competition's shared program frame (used across the corpus per the inventory), not a team's creative property; our own program identity must be different.
- **"DZJB 91.26 KHz" (and the whole invented-callsign + 91.26 kHz pattern)** — the 91.26 prop recurs across many teams; inventing our own unique callsign/frequency identity is required, and this entry's on-air garbling ("TVC JB", "21.26 KHz") shows the risk of under-rehearsed IDs.
- **The taglines "Naninindigan para sa katotohanan" and "Serbisyo at malasakit para sa bayan"** — generic-sounding but still this team's pairing; write our own values line.
- **Sign-off tags "Razada sa Sports Balita" and "all-in sa Balitang Trending"** — unique personal tags; we need our own per-reporter catchphrases.
- **"Isang mensahe, wala sa DMP lang ito."** — unintelligible in both source versions; no meaning to copy, and a caution that any slogan must survive a pronunciation check.
- **"Aarang ka na ang mga balita" / "Pupandana"** duo lines — the catchphrase and the unrecoverable unison line are this team's signature; our duo needs its own one-liner.
